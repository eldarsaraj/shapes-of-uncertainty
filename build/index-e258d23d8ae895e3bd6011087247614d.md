## What this (text)book is about

Perhaps I don't have to tell you that uncertainty is a fundamental aspect of our lives. From the moment you wake up in the morning and gather your first thoughts, to the moment you drift away in sleep, cushioned in your comfy bed, you're engulfed in the world without any guarantees.

You don't know if you'll wake up the next day. If you lucky and you do, you have no idea what will happen. Will you be happy, experience joy and success, or will your existence be marred by misery and misfortune? Will you achieve your goals? Will your actions yield results?

None of that can be known to us. That's simply the nature of the world we live in.

Uncertainty is not a glitch in an otherwise perfectly smooth reality. It's also not a temporary problem caused by missing information. It is a _structural feature_ of reality. It's inescapable. Nothing we try to do leads to any definite and certain outcome. Get out of the house and try to catch a train: sometimes you will catch it, sometimes you won't. Sometimes you'll wait 5 minutes, sometimes half an hour. Nothing is perfetly certain, even in the proverbially precise Switzerland.

While we're at the mercy of this whimsical lord every moment of our waking lives, it doesn't mean we are completely powerless. Fortunately, there are ways to reduce our uncertainty and at least have some idea about what might happen next, so our actions are not completely in the dark.

Those ways are the main topic of this book.

The path towards reducing uncertainty leads through the understanding of patterns the randomness of our daily experiences manifests itself. These patterns (we will call them _shapes_) are everywhere and the aim of this book is to help you recognize them, understand their nature and prepare strategies to ensure that when you do things in life (from crossing the street to choosing a career), you know what you should and shouldn't expect.

The term professionals (scientists, statisticians, researchers, and philosophers) use for these 'shapes' is <span class="highlight">probability distributions</span>. This piece of jargon hides an amazingly complex, yet extremely useful lesson about the reality we share: there is not a single uncertainty out there, but many of them (thus it would be more appropriate to call them 'uncertainties', using a plural rather than a singular).

Once you learn to recognize the shape of the uncertainty you are facing, many familiar confusions dissolve. You will be able to act in uncertain situations with a much greater understanding of what are you doing. You'll be able to predict the most likely outcomes with greater confidence. And, you'll also learn to distinguish situations worth the risk from those that aren't.

Probability distribution is a staple concept in science. There is not a single scientist in the world worth the name who is not aware of it's meaning and purpose. Understanding probability distributions helps scientists conduct experiments, derive conclusions from them, and make distinctions between mere accidents and meaningful discoveries.

However, the public at large is fairly ignorant of this concept and it's fundamental importance, not just for science but also for critical thinking in general. This book aims to change that. It will use probability distributions as tools for understanding the world available to (and beneficial for) everybody. The chapters that follow will simplify complex scientific methods of dealing with uncertainty into actionable lessons we all can use to make better decisions, avoid mistakes, and improve our lives.

In other words, the book will help you apply the cutting edge scientific reasoning to everyday problems in your life where critical thinking is called for.

## About the author

I am a critical thinking professor and my main expertise is philosophy. While I love math and statistics, this is not a mathematical book. It is primarily a work in (analytic) philosophy, a discipline known for prizing clarity, decomposition, and an emphasis on precise problem solving steps and solutions.

I'm also a human being (go figure) affected by randomness and it's servants. As a child I experienced a brutal ethnic war in my home country of Bosnia, when my life took an unexpected turn. I could have never imagined that I will one day be teaching philosophy in downtown Manhattan, rubbing shoulders with students, Wall street finance guys, and survivors of 9/11 alike.

I dedicate this book to them.
